How to tackle any question

Two pictures to keep in your head. The first is the route: seven steps, in order, with a time budget. The second is the drawing you make at step 5, with the lessons behind each box. Both are editable in Excalidraw.

The route: seven steps, 45 minutes

Say each step out loud

Interviewers are not grading the final design. They are grading how you get there. Walking this route in order, announcing each step, is most of the score. The minutes are a guide, not a rule: spend extra time where the question is hard, and skip faster through what is obvious.

2

The picture you draw at step 5

Lessons 19–22 on one page

Almost every question ends up as some version of this drawing. Draw it left to right while talking: a request comes in from the client, passes the load balancer, reaches a stateless API server, and from there touches the cache, the database, the file store or the queue. Then pick the boxes that matter for this question and go deep (step 6).

Sketch 2 · the step-5 pictureDownload for Excalidraw

How to use the picture in step 7

Walk it again from left to right and ask two questions at every box: “What if this dies?” and “What if traffic is 10× tomorrow?” The notes give the answer for the stateful boxes; for the stateless ones the answer is “add more of them behind the load balancer.”

Step 2

Open it on excalidraw.com

Go to excalidraw.com, open the menu (☰, top left) → Open, and pick the file. Or just drag the file onto the canvas.

Step 3

Practise on a copy

For each practice question, duplicate the step-5 picture and change it: remove boxes you do not need, add the ones the question needs, and write the numbers from step 2 next to the arrows.

Tip

In a real interview you draw on a shared whiteboard tool, often Excalidraw itself. Practising the drawing until it takes under three minutes is worth more than memorising any single design.
